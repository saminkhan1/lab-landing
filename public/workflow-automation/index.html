fit

Useful when repeated computer work is slowing down the business.

This is for solo owners, founders, operators, finance leads, admin teams, service firms, and anyone else who keeps doing the same kind of task across tabs, files, and business systems.

A strong first task

  • You or your team repeat the same kind of computer work daily, weekly, or every time a customer, invoice, order, vendor, or report appears.
  • The work jumps between tabs, forms, files, emails, portals, spreadsheets, or internal tools.
  • A person can show how they do it today, even if the starting material is messy.
  • The result can be checked: a record is updated, a file is saved, a report is downloaded, a draft is prepared, or an exception is flagged.
  • Important actions can wait for approval instead of being pushed through automatically.

Probably not worth it yet

  • The task is a one-off project that will not happen again.
  • It changes so much every time that there is no pattern to learn or check.
  • It takes only a minute and is not blocking higher-value work.
  • It depends entirely on personal judgment, negotiation, taste, or a sensitive decision.
  • You want risky actions like payments, legal filings, payroll changes, or account shutdowns to happen without review.

control

A helper should save time without taking away control.

The goal is not to let software make every decision. The goal is to remove repeated computer work while keeping people responsible for approvals, exceptions, and judgment calls.

Every run should be inspectable.

Owners and operators do not just need speed. They need to know what happened, where the helper paused, and what a person needs to decide next.

  • Logs and screenshots show the actions taken across the tools you already use.
  • Checks compare expected fields, files, and page states before moving on.
  • Approvals gate irreversible actions such as submissions, account changes, or payment-adjacent steps.
  • Exceptions route to a person instead of forcing the helper through an uncertain moment.
  • Broken tasks are updated from the demonstrated path rather than rebuilt from scratch.

questions

What business task should you show us?

Start with the task you keep delaying, delegating, or repeating while better work waits. We care less about whether it is tidy and more about whether the pattern repeats.

Is this useful if I work alone?

Yes. A solo owner or founder can be a strong fit when the same computer task keeps interrupting sales, service, finance, operations, or customer work.

Does the task need to be perfectly organized?

No. The task can start from a messy inbox, a PDF, a portal login, a spreadsheet, or notes in someone's head. We just need a person to show the usual path.

What can the helper do?

It can open pages, fill forms, copy values, compare files, upload documents, download reports, prepare drafts, flag exceptions, and pause for review before important actions.

When is it not worth automating?

It is probably not worth it when the task is a one-off, takes only a minute, changes completely every time, or depends almost entirely on judgment, taste, negotiation, or sensitive decisions.
