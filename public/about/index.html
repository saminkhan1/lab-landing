Operating principle: make the scope inspectable, define the result before implementation, and measure verified outcomes instead of treating activity as proof.

The approach

Start with one bounded problem and an explicit result.

01

Map the real path

Document the applications, handoffs, checks, exceptions, and judgment points in one representative case instead of designing from an idealized process diagram.

02

Choose the strongest connection

Prefer reliable APIs when they expose the needed action. Evaluate browser, desktop, legacy, and file-based steps when that is where the work actually happens.

03

Measure a controlled pilot

Agree on eligibility, verified completion, review burden, runtime, and cost before increasing volume. Keep exclusions and failures visible.

The evidence standard

Public claims should be narrower than the proof behind them.

  • Examples: workload calculations are labeled illustrative unless they come from an attributable measured case.
  • Methods: definitions, denominators, formulas, and decision boundaries are published so readers can inspect the reasoning.
  • Results: a customer result should identify the workflow, sample, measurement window, exceptions, and method before it is presented as evidence.
  • Limits: a pilot does not guarantee that excluded cases are solved or that performance remains unchanged at higher volume.
